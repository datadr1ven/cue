What we alert on

TPlus watches a launch webcast and posts major flight milestones — not a second-by-second countdown. Which events appear depends on that mission’s published timeline. Schedules and streams can lag; this project is unofficial.

On a typical flight

You get

  • Liftoff
  • Max-Q, main-engine cutoff / stage separation, fairing deploy — when listed for the mission
  • Boostback, entry, and landing burns
  • Booster landing, splashdown, or catch
  • Payload deploy start and complete
  • Hold, scrub, or go-for-launch when those are called

You don’t

  • Every countdown second
  • Every camera cut or on-screen graphic change
  • Guaranteed mid-flight milestones when the public timeline only has liftoff
  • Official range or launch-provider status

Common questions

Why only liftoff on some flights?
Mission timelines are imported from Launch Library 2. Some missions publish a launch window and webcast with few or no timed events after liftoff. TPlus does not invent milestones that are not on the plan.
What does an “audio” note on an alert mean?
Optional speech recognition can corroborate a milestone (for example hearing a callout). The on-screen mission clock and the published plan remain the primary triggers.
I saw something on the webcast that never alerted — why?
Public alerts generally follow the mission plan. Events that are not listed there, or that were not marked by the operator during the flight, usually stay off the subscriber feed on purpose.

How detection is evolving

Today, alerts are driven mainly by the webcast’s mission clock and the published timeline. The longer-term design is to combine several supporting signals from the same stream — speech, video, and audio cues — so each milestone is corroborated rather than guessed from a single detector.
